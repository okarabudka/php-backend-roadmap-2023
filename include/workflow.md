## How the team worked [&uarr;](#ticketing-systems-and-task-organization)

We used the Waterfall methodology (cascade model).

- Waterfall is a project management approach with a strict sequence of stages and no backtracking.
- Agile is a system of ideas and principles for flexible project management. It led to popular methods like Scrum and
  Kanban. The key principle is delivery in short iterations (cycles), each ending with working code or product.

Agile became the basis for several flexible methods, including Scrum, Lean, and Extreme Programming.

- Scrum is an Agile-based framework built around sprints (1-4 weeks), each delivering a working product increment.
- Lean grew from the Toyota Production System. It focuses on continuous improvement and maximizing customer value.
- Extreme Programming (XP) is an Agile methodology that emphasizes frequent planning games with the customer to adjust
  priorities, scope, and feedback.

In my opinion, Agile is better for development, while Waterfall works better for maintenance.

## Conflict resolution methods [&uarr;](#ticketing-systems-and-task-organization)

Common conflict types in teams:

- person-to-person (interpersonal),
- person-to-group,
- group-to-group (intergroup).

Key reasons include emotional factors, conflicting goals, manipulative behavior, and clashes of interests.

Key skills for conflict management:

- Identify the conflict source: different views, values, and priorities.
- Build a connection between sides: find shared goals and willingness to compromise.
- Establish constructive dialogue: recognize aggression and emotions; keep mutual respect and aim for agreements.
- Create positive ties: improve communication to focus on solutions and goals.

## Understanding Scrum and its attributes (standups, grooming/backlog estimation, retrospectives) [&uarr;](#ticketing-systems-and-task-organization)

Scrum is one of the most popular software development frameworks. It provides a structure to solve problems and build
valuable products efficiently.

Scrum has three core roles:

- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Development Team

The Product Owner connects the development team and the customer. Their job is to maximize product value.

The Product Backlog is the primary tool for the Product Owner. It contains prioritized work items (stories, bugs,
tasks) ordered by importance.

The Scrum Master is a servant-leader who helps the team remove impediments, improve skills, and stay motivated, while
also supporting the Product Owner.

The Development Team consists of specialists who build the product.

The recommended team size is 7 (plus or minus 2). Larger teams require more communication overhead, while smaller teams
increase risk due to missing skills and reduce throughput.

### Scrum process

Scrum is built around the Sprint, during which the product is developed. Each Sprint ends with a working increment.
Sprints are time-boxed (1-4 weeks) and remain consistent across the product lifecycle.

Before each Sprint, the team holds Sprint Planning to evaluate the Product Backlog and create a Sprint Backlog. Each
Sprint has a goal that motivates the team and is achieved through Sprint Backlog tasks.

Every day, the team holds a Daily Scrum where each member answers:

- What did I do yesterday?
- What will I do today?
- What obstacles did I face?

The goal is to assess progress, surface blockers early, and adjust the plan as needed.

At the end of the Sprint, the team runs a Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective to evaluate results, productivity,
problems, and expectations for the next Sprint.

# Resume Info

## General advice

- Size. Ideal resume length is 1 page for < 10 years of experience or if you have no open source contributions. If you
  can cut to 1 page, do it. Reduce bullet points (items in work experience). Try to keep 3-4 per role if you have more.
  Check whether your experience is relevant. I also recommend putting skills at the top.
- Style. The resume should be consistent. Do not mix italic, bold, different fonts, sizes, and colors.
- One column. Do not split your resume into two columns. The right column pulls attention to less valuable information.
- Two columns also work poorly with older ATS. Use a boring single-column layout.
- ATS parsing. Try copying the resume text into a plain text editor. Does it stay readable?
- Format: PDF. Some companies may have a different corporate standard, but you will know if they do.
- Composition. Left-align the text. It reads well (like an article or book).
- If you apply to different roles (for example DS/DA), use a separate CV for each role with relevant experience. Make a
  base resume, but do not send it as-is. Find a job you like, list their requirements, and if you have them, add them to
  your resume and send the tailored version. So for DRY, KISS, etc: if the job requires it, add it; if not, do not.
- File name should be in the format: first name, last name, CV, with any separators. Example: `Alex_Ivanov_CV.pdf`.
  Avoid `_Final`, `_v42`.

- First name first, last name second (example: Alex Ivanov).
- Make links clickable. Check that they work. Remove them if they lead nowhere (no GitHub projects, no link).

- Personal Summary: the summary section can be included in your CV. It should briefly describe your skills, experience,
  and credentials. It is not detailed; it introduces the rest of the CV.
- Make the summary shorter but more meaningful. Say what value you can bring to the new company based on your
  experience.
- Summary is a solid block of text. It should not be a list of bullets.

- The SKILLS section is important for technical roles. It should be near the top, right under the summary. For
  readability, split it into Languages and Frameworks (or similar). Do not dump the entire alphabet soup. Put your
  strongest skills first.
- I would not list items like "prettier, eslint, jira, git" as technical skills. It makes you look like a beginner.
- List technologies that appear in most job postings. Compare your resume with mid/senior examples.
- A huge skill list only shows insecurity. Make a short grouped list. Remove long lists of APIs, ORMs, management, and
  architectures.

- Work experience: list in reverse chronological order. Include dates and location, and be clear about the industry and
  role. Use strong, active language and bullets for achievements. Account for employment gaps.
- Shorten the list of tasks at each job (usually 2-3 bullets, 4-5 if there is a lot). Add experience outside the project
  (published libraries, pull requests to existing projects, interesting personal projects).
- Experience: 6-10 words about the company is enough. In bullet points, describe the impact you made, not the process.
  Everyone writes code and integrates APIs. Tell what was left after you.
- Look at your work not as reports, but as a set of changes: what was before you joined and what changed after.
- Write a sales-style description for each company so industry expertise and team scale are clear.
- Do not list responsibilities like "Analyzing product and marketing metrics". Focus on achievements.
- Remove soft skills from the skills list. Show them via impact and in interviews.

- COVER LETTER: do not waste time unless you have the hiring manager or recruiter name and email and can send directly.
  Cover letters should be unique for each position, so do not write them unless you are sure someone will read them.
- Cover letter: try 5 applications with a cover letter and 50 without, then compare effectiveness. Or a week without a
  letter and a week with one.
- How important is a cover letter? If a company wants to learn something about you, they will add a questionnaire.
- If you decide to write a cover letter, do it well. Address it personally and spend an hour. What do you lose? Or keep
  clicking on random postings hoping for crumbs.

- GitHub is the face of a developer. Treat it like a second resume with a photo. Improve your main
  profile README so it looks polished.
- Empty GitHub is pointless to show on a resume. No project descriptions, no results.
- But honestly, most people do not care about GitHub and code. The funnel is resume and interview.

## US resume format

- Personal information: remove photo, gender, age, marital status. It relates to discrimination.
- Add 3-4 bullet points with expanded description using XYZ format and numbers: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by
  doing [Z]".
- Remove responsibilities from descriptions. Do not say you wrote code or fixed bugs. Be specific.
- Remove hobbies and the story of how you got into the field.
- I would remove language level. If you apply to English-speaking companies, you imply your English is sufficient for
  communication.

## German resume format

- If your German is weak, do not write the resume in German. Do not mislead expectations.
- Photo in the top right, passport style.
- Age: include it so your profile is complete.
- Near the top of your CV, include your name, home address, phone number, email address, and date of birth.
- You can also include place of birth, marital status, and number of children (if any).
- Education should be in reverse chronological order, all the way back to high school. Each entry should include the
  institution name and area of study.

## Advice for anyone writing their own resume

- Job postings are a compromise of requirements inside companies. You do not have to meet 100% of requirements.
- Your resume is a landing page. Its job is not to guarantee an offer, but to convert views into a screening call.
  Selling is not deception. It is a description of what employers want for their business based on what you can do.
- A resume praises you. It highlights strengths and hides weaknesses.
- The main goal of hiring: find a candidate with the highest ROI. The company earns money from customers. It wants to
  earn more and spend less.
- Use AI. Ask it to suggest relevant KPIs, OCR, product, business, and technical metrics for your business model. If you
  do not know where the company makes money, describe it and ask AI to determine what is valuable for the business.
- All information is available. The hardest part is to make yourself prepare. No one can do it for you.
- On Reddit, salary questions are normal. You can get great info on market numbers and what they pay for.
- Before moving to Germany, if I asked "is this salary normal for Germany", people would spend days explaining that
  even cafes do not pay that. It was demotivating. Instead, research the market via LinkedIn, Levels, Glassdoor, and
  specific contacts. Also check cost of living on sites like Numbeo (data can lag by 1-2 years, which can skew the
  picture). Compare with the lifestyle level you want at the start.
- I have not heard that university years should be removed. It is only if you fear age discrimination; sometimes people
  recommend hiding them.
- I would remove "middle". In the US there is no "middle". They just write "software engineer".
- In Germany there is still a shortage of specialists, teams often hire people from India, Portugal, Russia, and more.
  In most companies I know, the internal language is English, especially inside the dev team.

Will be seen as a clear minus:

- A gap in work history
- Less than 2 years in a line role
- Less than 4 years in a leadership role
- A demotion (from team lead to developer, for example)
- More than 8 years in one place
- No title growth over 5+ years
